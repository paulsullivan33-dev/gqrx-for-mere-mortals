The lab — one synthetic recording, no antenna, no sound card
========================================================================

This guide's lab is a file of samples that was
never in the air, plus a build machine with no
receiver and no audio device attached. Both
halves are deliberate, and both are stated here
so nothing in the chapters can pretend
otherwise.

THE RECORDING
--------------------
tone.cf32 — four seconds of complex float32
I/Q at 1,000,000 samples per second, made by
make-lab-file.py (beside this note): a tone of
amplitude 0.5 at +120,000 Hz and a tone of
amplitude 0.15 at -200,000 Hz over light
Gaussian noise. 32,000,000 bytes, 4,000,000
samples. Regenerate it with:

    python3 make-lab-file.py

The generator is seeded, so your copy is
byte-identical to the guide's. Everything the
chapters verify by measurement rests on those
two tones and two sums:

    20 * log10(0.5)        = -6.02 dBFS
        (the strong tone, as STRENGTH reads it)
    20 * log10(0.15 / 0.5) = -10.46 dB
        (the weak tone, relative to the strong
        one, in an FFT of GQRX's own recording)

THE BUILD MACHINE
--------------------
Ubuntu 24.04 under a virtual display (Xvfb),
no RTL-SDR dongle, no antenna, no sound card.
Packages installed from the Ubuntu archive
purely to be run:

    gqrx-sdr    2.17.4-2build3
    gnuradio    3.10.9.2-1.1ubuntu2
    gr-osmosdr  0.2.0 (as the program reports)

GQRX was launched and driven through its own
menus and dialogs with synthetic input events;
screenshots were the working captures. Its
audio went to a PulseAudio null sink
(module-null-sink) after its first, captured
refusal to start without one: audio was
routed, counted, and recorded — never heard,
because there was nothing to hear it with.

GQRX's input for the whole session was the
recording above, through its own file device:

    file=/tmp/tone.cf32,freq=100e6,rate=1e6,repeat=true,throttle=true

CONSEQUENCES, STATED ONCE
--------------------
No signal was received over the air at any
point in this build, and no chapter reports
one. The chapters teach from the captures what
can honestly be taught from them — the
window's anatomy, the dialogs, the protocol,
the recorders' outputs measured afterwards —
and mark the rest (real reception, hardware
gain controls, RDS decoding, the UDP stream,
the other platforms) as documented-only.
criteria.md keeps the full ledger.

One housekeeping note: a second application
shared the build's virtual display and twice
took the keyboard focus, so two clicks meant
for menus landed on GQRX's spectrum instead
and retuned it (14.2885 MHz, then 14.3382 MHz).
The chapters report both where they matter —
one of them is preserved forever in the lab's
only bookmark — rather than tidying them out
of the record.
